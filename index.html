PART I — DECISION

1 · RECOMMENDATION

✔ PROCEED — final bid of $520MM enterprise value, fixed, submitted 21 Aug 2026

11.7x the corrected $44.6MM base and 10.0x the seller’s marketing number. Structure per §12: $212.0MM Ledgerock Credit unitranche at 4.75x, $88.0MM founder rollover (26.6% of the equity), $243.4MM Fund IV equity. Organic base of 2.42x / 19.4% with the value-creation program at §9 delivered; tuck-in M&A at the 5–6x multiples this market still clears adds an estimated +0.4–0.5x and is deliberately not underwritten. The bid may flex to $560MM only against full exclusivity, the Dr. Kolstad rollover executed at signing, and the Bluepine rate letter in hand — the three things that de-risk the two dated events in the downside calendar.

✘ DECLINE — above $560MM, and at the $665–700MM guidance frame at any structure

Above $560MM the base case falls through 2.2x and the price begins to underwrite the seller’s plan rather than the diligence record. At the frame, three facts are dispositive: (1) our base returns 1.57x; (2) the seller’s plan delivered in full returns 2.40x — still below target; (3) the downside returns 0.60x. A price that needs the best case to reach the target return has no owner of the downside. If the process clears above $560MM, the correct outcome is to lose.

Why this is the frame’s problem and not ours

Whitcombe & Ellery built the guidance on the $52.0MM marketing base and a precedent set drawn from scaled, multi-specialty DSOs with proven de novo engines (§11). CDP is neither yet — it is a superb regional platform with one hero state and a development engine still being industrialized. Paying the scaled-platform multiple on the pre-scale asset hands the seller the value-creation plan Harborlight would spend five years delivering. At $520MM, the sponsor is paid to build; at $695MM, the sponsor pays for the building it has not yet done.

3 · THE THREE EQUITY QUESTIONS — ANSWERED

Every services roll-up turns on three questions. Here they are answered with the evidence, and each answer prices directly into the bid.

Q1 — Is the growth the patients’ or the acquisitions’? Mostly the patients’, once you cut it honestly: +6.7pts of the +11.2pts is organic — same-clinic +4.8 (visits +2.9%, revenue per visit +1.9%) and de novo ramp +1.9 — verified to the practice-management system. The remaining +6.8pts is FY24–25 acquisitions annualizing through the base and does not repeat without new deals; closures and exited contracts cost −2.3pts. The base case carries the organic engine and retires the acquired layer — which is why our FY27 growth is +6.3% against a seller plan of +11.6%. One honest caveat inside the organic number: ~1.1pts of the same-clinic line was a Q3-25 hygiene reactivation blitz, quantified and removed at §4.

Q2 — Is the 18–21% margin structural or a staffing artifact? Structural, with a wage and payor caveat. Clinical wages are the real headwind — hygienists +9% and assistants +7% over two years — and the margin held anyway on revenue per visit (+1.9%), procurement, and specialty mix, not on running chairs short: staffing sits at 92% of model and the QoE strips the margin that came from pretending vacant seats were filled. The FY26→FY31 bridge at §9 is +350bps gross of a −100bps wage headwind — every lever named, owned and dated. The margin risk that matters is not the clinic P&L; it is the payor-rate calendar in the downside, and it is modeled at §8.

Q3 — Who buys this from us, and at what number? Both strategics and sponsors — if we build the two things the current asset lacks: revenue scale past ~$300MM and a proven multi-specialty, multi-state operating record. The precedent set at §11 splits cleanly: scaled, multi-specialty DSOs with de novo engines cleared 12.4–14.2x; regional GP-heavy chains cleared 9.2–10.4x. Our base exits at 11.25x — between the shelves, earned by FY29 scale, not assumed. The exit thesis is the value-creation plan; they are the same document.

The read

At $520MM this is a 2.42x / 19.4% underwrite on the organic engine, with the roll-up owned free. The bridge is 72% EBITDA growth, the exit multiple is assumed to contract, and leverage at 4.75x is a financing choice, not a return strategy — the covenant walk shows why. The sensitivity grid is the only exhibit the IC strictly needs: hold the operations constant and walk the price, and the deal passes at our bid, strains at our ceiling, and fails at the frame in every column that doesn’t require believing the seller’s plan and a premium exit simultaneously.

20 · METHODOLOGY & ENGAGEMENT STANDARD

The standard: every figure in this package is one of four things — verified (tied to a native source: file, tab, cell), seller (their claim, displayed and labeled, never adopted), external (purchased or fielded outside the room, used for triangulation only), or model (our construction, assumptions stated where used). Tags travel with the numbers. Where evidence was absent, the package says so and prices the gap — a flagged gap beats a guess, and both beat a blended average of the two.

Independence: seller claims were tested, never averaged. The two places the record improved on the seller’s story — the GPO supplies credit at §4.2 and the Medicaid-rate confirmation at §2.1 — are displayed with the same weight as the nine places it did not. That symmetry is the product.
