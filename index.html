Q2 expanded — the number the seller does not show

The seller’s headline metric is 94% “cash conversion.” Its own definition (CIM p52 n.6) is EBITDA less maintenance capex, over EBITDA — de novo capex ($10.2MM), the credentialing AR build ($1.8MM per opening cohort), PMS Phase 2 ($2.4MM) and integration one-times are all outside it. Q3 expanded — why downside value is thin exactly when it is needed

The platform cannot be separated from its payor mix. Four-wall value is a function of the fee schedules that produce it: at the FY28 trough the same 84 clinics that support $520MM on plan support $318MM at a distressed 8.5x on $37.4MM — and a forced process at the trough is exactly when the buyers know it. The watch-list is negative value in a workout (closure costs exceed working-capital recapture); the de novo book sells at cost or below because a distressed buyer pays for ramp, not plan; the PC/MSO structure adds transfer friction in every state, and the Summitcrest gap (F4) adds it twice. Sixty-two percent of clinicians are credentialed in one metro, so the people risk and the collateral concentration are the same fact. That is why §8 runs two lenses — a forced stress-exit and a going-concern sum-of-parts — and why neither is averaged with the other: at $495–520MM the worst honest outcome is a recoverable 0.9x held / 0.3x forced; at the frame the same facts read 0.6x held / 0.15x forced. Price is the only downside protection that does not depend on execution.
